WHY IT CHANGES THE WORK
Boiler or forced air
Many greystones and courtyard buildings still heat with steam or hot-water boilers. A boiler repair is a different job from a furnace one.
Cooling without ducts
Radiator-heated buildings have no ducts for central air, so cooling usually means ductless mini-splits or high-velocity systems.
Basement and alley access
Boilers sit in low basements; condensers go in gangways or on garage roofs off the alley. Access sets the labour.
Shared systems
In two-flats and condo conversions, one boiler can serve several units, so the association decides on repairs.
Lakefront high-rises
Fan coils on a building loop, with management approval, a certificate of insurance and booked elevator time.
Repair or replace
The biggest swing of all. A well-kept boiler can run for decades; where it has years left, we say so.
HOW TO CHOOSE
Five questions that sort Lincoln Park HVAC companies
Ask them of anyone you call, including us.
Q1
Who diagnoses, and who quotes?
If they're different people, ask who owns the outcome. Here it's Rob for both.
Q2
Is the technician on commission?
Commission on replacements changes what "your furnace is done" means.
Q3
Does a permit count mean anything here?
Not for repairs. Chicago doesn't permit HVAC repair or maintenance, so a permit count only measures installs.
Q4
What do the newest reviews say?
Read the last 20, not the top 5. Look for names and specifics.
Q5
Will they tell you to keep it?
Ask if the unit has years left. Our reviewers record hearing "yes".

THE PERMIT RECORD, HONESTLY
Why you won't find us in the City's permit file
Chicago doesn't require a permit to repair, diagnose or maintain heating and cooling, which is most of what we do. The permit file measures construction, not service. So when a company waves a permit count at you, ask how much of their work is repair.
