AThe ten non-negotiables

  1. Two brand colours, two jobs. Red #DD382B = act: every button, every eyebrow, the urgent "No heat in January?" note, and the two statement bands (Services, Permit record). Navy #09263A = trust: headings, the utility bar, dark bands, stat tiles and the highlighted "this is us" cards. Blue #186AA4 is only for information: "Read on Google" links, "HVAC here" notes and the selected district card.
  2. Red never sits on red. On the red bands, buttons and pills flip to white. On navy and on the hero photo, the secondary button is a 1.5px white outline with a white label.
  3. Two fonts, no third. Ubuntu 700 for headings, numbers and buttons; Ubuntu 500 for nav and chips. Noto Sans for body, labels and forms. Headings are sentence case; only eyebrows, buttons and small tags are uppercase.
  4. Every action is a pill. Buttons are radius-full, Ubuntu 700 uppercase with +0.04em tracking: red fill on white and navy, white fill on red.
  5. Radii are 8 / 16 / 24 / full. 8 inputs, 16 service tiles and notes, 24 cards and panels, full for buttons, tags and chips. Nothing else.
  6. Shadows only on floating cards. The trust strip over the hero and the services panel over the red band are the only elements with a shadow. Everywhere else, depth = a change of surface or a 1px --color-line border.
  7. The owner is the proof. Rob is named in the hero lede, the "Who you're calling" block and the process. The promise on every page: the person who diagnoses your system is the person who quotes it.
  8. Never invent a price, a number or a review. Prices are permit medians labelled "median reported value" and sourced to City data. Reviews are quoted word for word from Google. Missing facts stay as a [CONFIRM] note until verified.
  9. Be honest about the permit record. Chicago doesn't permit HVAC repair, so City & Suburban's own count is shown as 0, every time. Never hide or round that number.
  10. Keep the band rhythm. Sections alternate white and fog, punctuated by navy (Scope and price) and red (Services, Permit record). Two fog bands, two navy bands or two red bands never touch.

B4Reviews googleReviews

FCopy & voice rules

  • Plain, specific, local. Name the equipment ("steam boilers in converted warehouses") and the place ("Randolph and Fulton Market"). No "top-rated", "best-in-class" or "one-stop shop".
  • Repair first. Every page repeats the promise: diagnosis first, and where the unit has years left, we say so.
  • The owner by name. "Rob, the owner" in the hero lede and in B3. Other names appear only as "names our reviewers remember".
  • Headline patterns: H1 "Heating and cooling repair in {area}, Chicago". Section H2s are statements or the buyer's question, in sentence case.
  • Honest numbers only: every figure has a source (Part G), and the permit count of 0 is never left out.
  • Keep the words, hide the length. Inactive service tabs, FAQ answers after the first, and the full review text stay in the HTML behind a click. Never load hidden copy with JavaScript after the page loads.
  • [CONFIRM] notes are editorial flags shown in the amber dashed style in drafts only. A published page contains none.

HBuild & responsive notes

  • Tokens: copy the :root block from the design system page. Never hard-code a brand hex that has a token.
  • Fonts: Google Fonts: Ubuntu 500/700 and Noto Sans 400/600/700. Nothing else loads.
  • Exact values: select the layer in the Paper template. Layer names match the block names in Part C. With Claude Code + Paper MCP, pull get_jsx / get_computed_styles for that node.
  • Interactive parts: service tabs and FAQ use real buttons or <details> with keyboard support. Review and district tracks scroll horizontally with scroll-snap.
  • Structured data: LocalBusiness (HVACBusiness) + Service + FAQPage (from B12) + BreadcrumbList.

Responsive (mobile is not drawn yet, so follow these rules):

  • < 768px: side gutter 16px, section padding 64px, H1 36–40px, H2 30–32px. Every split layout stacks: copy first, then the card or table.
  • B1: the request card drops below the hero copy, and the trust strip wraps into a 2 × 2 grid.
  • B2: the tab rail becomes a horizontal row of pills above the panel, and tiles go to one column.
  • B5, B7, B8, B10: horizontal scroll-snap rows of cards at about 80% width.
  • B6 factor table: label above text. B3 and B11 tiles: one column.

IWorkflow for every new area

  1. Brief: the area name and its boundaries, ZIPs, wards and community area.
  2. Research: housing stock, sub-districts, access constraints and the equipment that's typical there. Pull the area's 24-month HVAC permit count from City data.
  3. Reviews: pick verbatim Google reviews from the review workbook, ranked by equipment match. If none are eligible, leave the [CONFIRM] note.
  4. Write the per-area fields only (Part C, last column), following Part E and Part F.
  5. Design: duplicate the West Loop template artboard in Paper, rename it "C&S — {area} area page (desktop)" and swap the text. Don't restyle.
  6. Publish the preview to this site and add it to the page switcher.
  7. QC with Part J, clear every [CONFIRM], then publish.

KOpen decisions

  1. Licence number: the City of Chicago HVAC licence number isn't in any workbook. Pull it from the City registry before any page goes live.
  2. Reviews: both pages still have [CONFIRM] placeholders in B4. They need verbatim quotes from the review workbook.
  3. Spelling: the template uses "Licences" (British). Decide on US "Licenses" for a Chicago audience and apply it everywhere.
  4. Mobile: mobile frames aren't drawn in Paper yet. The Part H rules apply until they are.
  5. Districts eyebrow contrast: on the template, the B9 eyebrow ("The West Loop, district by district") is white on fog and effectively invisible. Fix it to red like every other eyebrow, in Paper and in the build.
  6. Hero photo: confirm the hero image is City & Suburban's own or licensed. If it could be read as their own work and isn't, add an "Illustrative photo" label.
